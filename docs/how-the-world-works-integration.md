# How The World Works Integration

Source folder: `how-the-world-works/`.

This folder is Qadam's esoteric edge corpus and private world-model foundation. Internally, Qadam treats this corpus as a directional prior about how power, incentives, narrative control, institutional behaviour, and hidden coordination may operate. It is not a live API feed and it is not public-facing marketing language. Operationally, its claims still need live corroboration before they can affect signal confidence or execution.

## Private Operating Premise

Qadam privately believes the world is not best understood through surface narratives alone. It assumes that official explanations, institutional incentives, financial flows, military posture, media framing, and collective psychology often diverge. The `how-the-world-works/` corpus is the internal lens that keeps Qadam suspicious of consensus and alert to second-order power moves.

This premise is foundational to Qadam's intelligence, but it is not allowed to bypass evidence. The system can believe the lens is directionally useful while still forcing every tradable implication through observable signatures, live data, the Signal Integrity Gate, and postmortem scoring.

## Why It Matters

Qadam's edge depends on seeing the world before markets converge on consensus. Normal data feeds tell Qadam what happened. This corpus helps Qadam ask why certain actors might behave that way, what narrative cover might be used, which institutions have incentives to hide or frame events, and what observable signatures would confirm or falsify a thesis.

In Qadam terms, this is useful because it adds:

- Power-structure maps: military, finance, media, institutions, intelligence, trade, and ideology.
- Narrative asymmetry: official story vs alternative story vs market pricing.
- Incentive modelling: who benefits, who absorbs cost, who controls timing, who has plausible deniability.
- Scenario generation: extreme but testable futures that can be converted into watchlists.
- Red-team reasoning: adversarial prompts that challenge comfortable consensus.
- Psychological market insight: fear, distrust, panic, social contagion, and belief formation as tradable sentiment forces.
- Chokepoint awareness: petrodollar, BRICS, maritime routes, food/energy flows, command-and-control structures.

| Registry | Treatment |

| Resource Registry | Include as `private_foundation`, `world_model`, and `esoteric_edge` references. |
| Knowledge Graph | Extract entities, claims, causal links, incentives, scenarios, and falsification tests. |
| Signal pipeline | Use as a foundational lens and red-team hypothesis engine; only corroborated implications can affect signal confidence. |

## Extraction Model

Each claim or frame should be decomposed into:

- `claim`: what the corpus says.
- `claim_type`: factual, causal, predictive, metaphysical, symbolic, narrative, or strategic.
- `confidence`: always low until corroborated.
- `actors`: states, institutions, companies, individuals, alliances.
- `mechanism`: how the claim says power or causality flows.
- `observable_signatures`: what Qadam would expect to see in live data if the claim were directionally useful.
- `live_sources_to_check`: ACLED, GDELT, FIRMS, AIS, Wingbits, FRED, BIS, SEC, Polymarket, Kalshi, options flow, RSS, Telegram, X, Reddit, etc.
- `market_channels`: prediction markets, crude oil, defence, silver, semiconductors, rates, FX, food, shipping, cyber, insurance.
- `status`: untested, contradicted, weakly supported, supported, useful-as-narrative, rejected.

## Guardrails

- Treat this corpus as Qadam's private worldview prior, not as public evidence.
- Never allow it to directly originate a trade.
- Never let metaphysical or symbolic claims enter a signal as factual evidence.
- Always separate narrative importance from factual confidence.
- Require live-source corroboration before a frame affects signal confidence.
- Use it to ask better questions, not to bypass the Signal Integrity Gate.
- Flag unsupported or extreme claims as `useful-as-narrative` unless evidence upgrades them.
